# Human Authorship and Originality

## Goal

Support a genuinely author-driven manuscript and reduce plagiarism risk without attempting to game AI detectors.

## Human-Authored Mode Rules

1. The author remains responsible for the scientific ideas, claims, interpretation, and final wording.
2. Prefer planning, critique, verification, and close editing over fresh prose generation.
3. When editing author text, preserve its meaning and terminology unless the author asks for a substantive change.
4. Do not add a new scientific claim merely because it sounds academically appropriate.
5. Do not add citations merely to make a paragraph look scholarly.

## Originality Rules

1. Do not copy sentences from source papers.
2. Do not reproduce distinctive sentence structures from one source with only word substitutions.
3. Do not copy figure captions, method descriptions, or contribution wording from related papers.
4. Use literature to understand prior work and support citations, then write from the author's own understanding and project evidence.
5. Quote only when a quotation is genuinely necessary and the venue permits it.

## Citation Discipline

Every literature-based claim should be attached to the most relevant source.

Do not:

- invent authors, titles, venues, years, DOIs, or arXiv IDs;
- cite a paper that was not checked;
- attribute a result to a paper without confirming it;
- use a citation as decoration without claim relevance.

## Natural Academic Voice

Prefer:

- concrete technical nouns;
- precise verbs;
- measured claims;
- varied but natural sentence structure;
- direct explanations of why a design choice exists;
- explicit acknowledgment of limits.

Avoid:

- inflated novelty language;
- repeated phrases such as "notably", "in recent years", "groundbreaking", or "significantly" when unnecessary;
- empty transitions;
- generic conclusions that are not connected to the experiments;
- claims that sound stronger than the evidence.

This is a writing-quality rule, not an AI-detector evasion strategy.

## Final Author Check

Before submission, the author should read the full paper without the assistant and confirm:

1. Every sentence expresses something they understand.
2. Every technical claim is something they can defend.
3. Every number can be traced to an experiment or source.
4. Every citation is real and relevant.
5. The wording sounds natural to them.
6. They accept responsibility for the final manuscript.
