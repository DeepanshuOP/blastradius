# Introduction Writing Guide

## Goal

Write the Introduction by reasoning backward from the contribution and then presenting the story forward.

## Backward Questions

1. What technical problem is being solved?
2. Why is there no well-established solution?
3. What are the actual contributions?
4. Why should those contributions address the challenge?
5. What new understanding or insight does the work provide?
6. Which prior methods and limitations naturally lead to this challenge?

## Forward Story

1. Introduce the task and application.
2. Explain the relevant prior methods.
3. Identify the unresolved technical challenge and its reason.
4. Introduce the proposed solution/pipeline.
5. Explain why it should work and what its technical advantages are.
6. Summarize experimental evidence.
7. State concise contributions.

## Paragraph Rules

- One paragraph, one message.
- The first sentence should make the paragraph's role clear.
- Make technical nouns self-contained.
- Make the transition from previous limitation -> technical challenge -> proposed solution explicit.
- Do not hide a simple method behind abstract language merely to create an impression of novelty.
- Keep every contribution claim tied to something the paper actually does.
